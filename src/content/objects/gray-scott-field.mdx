The pattern on Sekiya's front page is not a video or a shader trick. It is a Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion system, computed live in your browser, and it will replay identically from its seed.

## Human

Two virtual chemicals spread through a grid. One feeds the other; both diffuse at different rates. From those two rules — feed, kill, diffuse, react — the system grows coral, mitosis and maze patterns that were never drawn by anyone.

Drag the time axis. You are scrubbing the computation itself: backward to an earlier state, forward as it re-computes. Change the seed and the same rules grow a different world.

## Technical

The system integrates the Gray–Scott equations on a GPU grid, in your browser, at every visit:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = D_u \nabla^2 u - uv^2 + F(1 - u)$$

$$\frac{\partial v}{\partial t} = D_v \nabla^2 v + uv^2 - (F + k)v$$

The initial condition is generated from an integer seed by a deterministic PRNG. Every 64 steps the engine stores a snapshot and a 16×16 digest; the checksum shown in the console is a hash of that digest. Scrubbing backward restores the nearest snapshot and re-integrates forward — which is why replay is exact rather than approximate.

## Full

**Presets.** Coral $(F{=}0.0545, k{=}0.0620)$, Mitosis $(F{=}0.0367, k{=}0.0649)$, Maze $(F{=}0.029, k{=}0.057)$; $D_u = 0.16$, $D_v = 0.08$, $\Delta t = 1$, nine-point Laplacian.

**Engine.** WebGL2 ping-pong buffers, RGBA16F where the extension exists; grid 256² on desktop, 192² on coarse-pointer devices; adaptive steps-per-frame under load.

**Reproducibility.** Same seed, preset, grid size and browser build reproduce the trajectory and its checksum. Cross-browser floating-point identity is not claimed — the honest unit of determinism is one configuration.
